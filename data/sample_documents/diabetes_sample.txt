DIABETES — GENERAL EDUCATIONAL CLINICAL OVERVIEW
=================================================

1. GLUCOSE (FASTING PLASMA GLUCOSE)
------------------------------------
Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) is one of the primary indicators used to assess
glycemic status. General educational reference ranges commonly discussed in
diabetes literature include:

- Normal fasting glucose: below 100 mg/dL
- Impaired fasting glucose (pre-diabetes range): 100–125 mg/dL
- Diabetes diagnostic threshold (when confirmed): 126 mg/dL or higher on two
  separate tests

Elevated fasting glucose reflects the body's reduced ability to maintain normal
blood sugar levels, which may be associated with impaired insulin secretion,
insulin resistance, or both. In the context of a machine learning model for
diabetes prediction, glucose level is typically one of the strongest numeric
predictors because of its direct relationship with glycemic dysregulation.

Clinical note: A single elevated glucose reading is not sufficient for a
confirmed diabetes diagnosis. Repeated testing and clinical evaluation are
required.

2. BODY MASS INDEX (BMI)
------------------------
BMI is a widely used anthropometric measure calculated as weight (kg) divided
by height squared (m²). In diabetes-related literature, BMI is discussed in
the context of metabolic risk:

- Underweight: below 18.5
- Normal weight: 18.5 – 24.9
- Overweight: 25.0 – 29.9
- Obesity class I: 30.0 – 34.9
- Obesity class II: 35.0 – 39.9
- Obesity class III: 40.0 and above

Higher BMI values are associated with greater adipose tissue, which can
contribute to insulin resistance — a condition in which the body's cells
respond less effectively to insulin. Visceral adiposity in particular has been
linked to increased metabolic risk in general educational literature.

In a predictive model for diabetes, BMI is frequently among the top
contributing features due to the association between obesity and impaired
glucose regulation.

3. AGE AS A RISK FACTOR
------------------------
Advancing age is a well-recognized factor in discussions of type 2 diabetes
risk. As individuals age, physiological changes may affect insulin sensitivity
and pancreatic beta-cell function. General educational literature frequently
notes that the risk of type 2 diabetes increases in middle-aged and older
adults.

In machine learning models trained on clinical datasets, age often contributes
positively to predictions of diabetes, particularly when combined with other
metabolic risk indicators such as elevated BMI or glucose.

4. BLOOD PRESSURE (DIASTOLIC)
------------------------------
Diastolic blood pressure (the lower number in a blood pressure reading)
represents the pressure in the arteries when the heart is at rest between
beats. Hypertension — elevated blood pressure — frequently co-occurs with type
2 diabetes. Educational literature describes this relationship as part of the
broader cardiometabolic syndrome.

General reference ranges discussed in educational contexts:
- Normal diastolic BP: below 80 mm Hg
- Elevated / Stage 1 hypertension: 80–89 mm Hg
- Stage 2 hypertension: 90 mm Hg and above

In a diabetes prediction model, blood pressure may contribute as a correlated
metabolic marker, though its individual contribution to the prediction may vary
depending on the patient's overall clinical profile.

5. INSULIN
----------
Insulin is a hormone produced by the pancreatic beta cells that regulates
blood glucose by facilitating cellular uptake of glucose. In type 2 diabetes,
early stages may be characterised by hyperinsulinemia (excess insulin production
as a compensatory response to insulin resistance), while later stages may involve
reduced insulin secretion.

In fasting plasma measurements, insulin levels can reflect the degree of
insulin resistance. In predictive models, elevated fasting insulin combined
with elevated glucose may signal a higher probability of diabetes.

6. DIABETES PEDIGREE FUNCTION
------------------------------
The Diabetes Pedigree Function (DPF) is a synthetic score developed to estimate
the genetic influence of family history on diabetes risk. A higher DPF value
suggests stronger familial predisposition. It is a feature specific to the
Pima Indians Diabetes Dataset and related datasets.

In predictive models, DPF captures hereditary risk that may not be reflected
in metabolic measurements alone. It contributes to prediction by encoding
genetic predisposition in numeric form.

7. INSULIN RESISTANCE
---------------------
Insulin resistance is a physiological state in which target tissues respond
sub-optimally to insulin signalling. It is considered a central mechanism in
the development of type 2 diabetes. Educational literature describes insulin
resistance as influenced by factors including adiposity, physical inactivity,
chronic inflammation, and genetic predisposition.

The HOMA-IR (Homeostatic Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance) is one
commonly referenced metric in educational literature, calculated using fasting
insulin and fasting glucose.

8. GENERAL CLINICAL CONTEXT
----------------------------
Diabetes mellitus type 2 is a chronic metabolic condition characterised by
hyperglycaemia resulting from impaired insulin secretion and/or insulin
resistance. Educational literature identifies modifiable risk factors including
physical inactivity, unhealthy diet, overweight, and obesity. Non-modifiable
risk factors include genetics, age, and family history.

Clinical evaluation for diabetes typically involves:
- Fasting plasma glucose testing
- HbA1c (glycated haemoglobin) measurement
- Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) in some cases

A machine learning model that predicts diabetes based on clinical features
provides decision support only. It does not replace clinical evaluation,
laboratory testing, or physician judgement.
